# Task 1: Security Fundamentals
## Cybersecurity Internship — Skillzeno

**Task:** Security Fundamentals  
**Focus:** Common threats, scenario analysis, risk identification, and security awareness.

## 1. Objective
Study common cybersecurity threats, analyze realistic security scenarios, identify their risks, and recommend practical measures that reduce the likelihood and impact of security incidents.

## 2. Common Cybersecurity Threats

### 2.1 Phishing
Phishing is a social-engineering technique in which an attacker impersonates a trusted person or organization to trick someone into disclosing sensitive information or opening a malicious link or attachment.

**Example:** An email claims that a bank account will be suspended unless the recipient clicks a link and enters login details.

**Risks:** Credential theft, account takeover, identity theft, and financial loss.

**Prevention:** Check the sender and destination URL carefully; access services using official websites or apps; do not share passwords, OTPs, or recovery codes; report suspicious messages.

### 2.2 Malware
Malware is software designed to damage systems, disrupt operations, spy on users, or gain unauthorized access. Examples include viruses, trojans, spyware, worms, and ransomware.

**Example:** A person installs an application from an untrusted website that secretly collects information.

**Risks:** Data theft, system disruption, privacy loss, and unauthorized access.

**Prevention:** Install software from trusted sources, apply security updates, use reputable endpoint protection, maintain backups, and avoid unexpected attachments.

### 2.3 Weak or Reused Passwords
Short, common, predictable, or reused passwords make accounts easier to compromise. If one service exposes a reused password, attackers may try it on other services.

**Example:** Using the same password for email, social media, and an online shopping account.

**Risks:** Credential stuffing, account takeover, exposure of private data, and loss of access.

**Prevention:** Use long, unique passwords or passphrases, use a reputable password manager, and enable multi-factor authentication (MFA).

### 2.4 Social Engineering
Social engineering manipulates people into revealing information or taking actions that weaken security. It can occur over phone calls, messages, email, or in person.

**Example:** Someone pretending to be IT support asks an employee for their password or MFA code.

**Risks:** Unauthorized system access, disclosure of confidential data, fraud, and further compromise.

**Prevention:** Verify identities using known official channels, never disclose passwords or authentication codes, and follow established reporting procedures.

## 3. Sample Security Scenarios

### Scenario A: Suspicious Bank Email
**Situation:** A student receives an urgent email stating that their bank account will be blocked unless they follow a link and provide their password and OTP.

**Risks identified:**
- Theft of banking credentials.
- Unauthorized transactions or account takeover.
- Identity theft or exposure of personal information.

**Recommended response:** Do not click the link or reply with sensitive details. Open the bank's official app or manually enter its known website address. Report the email through the bank's official channel and delete it.

### Scenario B: Unexpected Software Installation
**Situation:** An employee downloads a free application from an unfamiliar site. After installation, unexpected pop-ups appear and the device behaves unusually.

**Risks identified:**
- Possible malware infection.
- Theft or exposure of files and credentials.
- Unauthorized activity on the affected device.

**Recommended response:** Stop using the suspicious application and avoid entering credentials on the potentially affected device. If compromise is suspected, disconnect it from networks and contact the authorized IT/security team. Scan and remediate it with trusted tools.

### Scenario C: Password Reuse
**Situation:** A student uses one password across several websites. One website later announces a data breach.

**Risks identified:**
- Attackers may try the exposed password on other services.
- Email or social accounts may be taken over.
- Private information may be accessed.

**Recommended response:** From a trusted device, change the exposed password anywhere it was reused, use unique passwords for all accounts, enable MFA, and review recent account activity.

### Scenario D: Fake IT Support Call
**Situation:** A caller claims to work for IT support and asks an employee to reveal their password and MFA code to fix an issue.

**Risks identified:**
- Credential and authentication-code theft.
- Unauthorized access to work systems.
- Potential compromise of organizational information.

**Recommended response:** Do not provide credentials or codes. End the call and contact IT through a verified internal directory or help desk. Report the incident according to organizational policy.

## 4. Risk Summary

| Scenario | Primary threat | Potential impact | Priority response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank email | Phishing | Financial loss and account takeover | Avoid the link; verify with the bank |
| Unknown application | Malware | Device and data compromise | Isolate if compromised; contact IT |
| Reused password | Credential stuffing | Multiple accounts exposed | Change reused passwords; enable MFA |
| Fake support call | Social engineering | Unauthorized access | Verify independently; report the call |

## 5. Security Awareness Checklist
- Use unique, long passwords and a reputable password manager.
- Enable MFA, preferably phishing-resistant MFA where available.
- Keep operating systems, browsers, and applications updated.
- Avoid unknown downloads, suspicious links, and unexpected attachments.
- Verify unusual requests for money, credentials, or sensitive information through a separate trusted channel.
- Back up important files and periodically check that backups can be restored.
- Lock devices when unattended and review account activity.
- Report suspected security incidents promptly and follow organizational procedures.
- Avoid sensitive activity on untrusted networks; use approved protections.
- Share information only with authorized recipients.

## 6. Conclusion
Phishing, malware, weak passwords, and social engineering can affect individuals and organizations. Recognizing warning signs, using strong authentication, keeping systems updated, verifying unexpected requests, and reporting incidents can reduce security risk. Security awareness is an ongoing responsibility that combines safe technology use with careful decision-making.
